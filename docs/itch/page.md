## Title

Say Yes to Soup

## Short description / tagline (73 chars)

Walk Nani's 1974 route through eleven villages. Say yes to every bowl.

## Description (paste into the main text box)

*a journal, half full*

You grew up on your grandmother's postcards. Then the postcards stopped. The lawyer's envelope held no money and one journal, half full. Her note said the empty half was always yours. So: unpaid leave, one bag, her 1974 route.

**Say Yes to Soup** is a cozy RPG that runs right here in your browser. Eleven villages, every hop a real connection: an Andean plaza, the Peruvian desert coast, a cargo ship across the Pacific, a Seto Inland Sea town at Tanabata, a Busan market lane, the Kerala backwaters at monsoon onset, the rooftops of Old Delhi at pigeon hour, a Zanzibar shore village, a Sicilian fishing town under Etna, a valley in Oaxaca at Dia de los Muertos, and the same road home, upward.

What you do in it is what she told you to do: **walk slowly. Cook when someone lets you. Learn the word before the dish. If someone corrects you, thank them twice.** No combat, no timers, no fail states. You can sit on any bench, and the place will think out loud around you.

- **Eleven villages**, each written from research, none of them a postcard cliche
- **Her journal is the quest log**: your entries stitch to hers, and margin notes in her hand become legible only when you hold both halves
- **Every picture is painted by the game as it runs**: no sprite sheets, no asset packs, and the music is generated live in each region's own style (bombo, taiko, janggu, chenda, taarab, tarantella, marimba)
- **Cooking that cares**: a watia earth oven, morning dashi, hotteok on a night market griddle, a meter-long chaya pour, cannoli filled at the moment and never before
- **Nothing to install**: it plays in the browser tab you are already in
- **6 to 9 hours** at the pace it wants you to keep, and it is free

**Accessibility:** fully playable with keyboard alone (arrows or WASD plus two buttons); mouse and touch are first class too. Settings include a text size option and reduced motion. Minigames are designed to be colorblind-safe: nothing hinges on color alone.
