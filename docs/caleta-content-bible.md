# Chapter Two content bible: La Caleta (Peruvian north coast)

Condensed from the sourced research pass of 2026-07-21 (full text with all
citations in the conversation log; source list summarized at bottom). Village
is fictional, in the Huanchaco tradition with texture from La Tortuga, El
Ñuro, Ancón; Chimbote is the industrial shadow.

## The operative facts (game-usable)

**Rhythm.** Launches before first light BUT no fixed schedule: "No hay un
horario exacto. Todo depende del mar." Waiting together on the sand is work
and sociability. Catch lands on the wet sand; wives/partners run the stall,
the logistics, the paperwork ("Ellos capturan. Nosotras administramos,
vendemos, sostenemos el muelle"). When nothing sells: rematar (slash prices
so everyone at least eats). Evenings: emoliente cart, nets mended, wet reed
boats stood upright on their tails along the promenade to drain overnight.

**Garúa.** June-Sept: pearl-grey fog ceiling, damp cold, halos on lights, sea
and sky one color; almost never actual rain (<10mm/yr). Summer: hard blue
glare, black noon shadows. Two lighting moods, gift-wrapped for our engine.

**Caballito de totora (the icon).** ~3,000-3,500 years old (Moche/Chimú
pottery shows them; Mochica name "tup"). 3-4m, ~40-50kg dry and double wet,
four bound reed bundles: two "madres," two "hijos"; built in ~2 hours by a
skilled maker; paddle is split Guayaquil cane used kayak-style; ridden
kneeling/astride. Reeds grown in wachaques: sunken ponds dug to the water
table at the desert's edge; cut green, sun-dried ~15-20 days. **A boat lasts
about a month of daily use: the boat is a consumable; the KNOWLEDGE is the
heirloom. A fisherman is always partway through his next boat.** ~40 riders
remain; riding the wave home is proto-surfing (Huanchaco = World Surfing
Reserve 2012); fishers' kids become surf instructors. 2025: sewage collapses
poisoned most wachaques (~30 of 80+ left); community dug 13 new ponds
together; first year no patacho could be built for San Pedrito. "Sin la
totora no hay caballito."

**Food law.** Ceviche: corvina etc., minutes-long lime "kiss" (post-1970s
Nikkei influence; before that: hours), red onion, ají; sides camote, choclo,
cancha, yuyo; leche de tigre served as its own glass. **Midday ONLY, noon-3pm,
tied to the dawn catch: evening ceviche = old fish = the perfect
gentle-friction beat.** Also: tiradito (sliced thin, no onion, Nikkei),
sudado (with chicha de jora splash in the north; folk hangover cure),
chicharrón de pescado, chupe (Quechua word), tortitas de choclo, chicha
morada, and the emolientero's dawn/night cart. The eatery is a PICANTERÍA:
communal table, enter past the pots, one dish per weekday, courtesy shot at
the end. No menus.

**The yapa / caserita loop (Chapter Two's ayni-rhyme).** casera/caserita =
mutual word for regular customer AND habitual vendor. Regulars earn the yapa:
the small free extra (from Quechua *yapay*, "to add"; became Louisiana's
"lagniappe"). Mechanic: you earn yapa by BECOMING a casero, not by paying.

**Language by ear (coastal ribereño).** pe (sentence-final), habla causa,
causa, pata, chévere/bacán, al toque, yapa, casero/caserita, buenazo, chamba,
jato, chela, chibolo, luca, ahorita(-ish). Fisher register: chalana, espinel,
cortina (gillnet), pinta (handline), faena; fish names AS vocabulary: lisa
(humble daily), lorna, bonito, corvina, caballa, perico. Quechua loans alive
in coastal mouths: yapa, cancha, choclo, chupe, palta: the highlands audible
inside coastal Spanish (bridge to Ch1).

**La mar.** Landsmen say "el mar"; those who work on her say "la mar,"
feminine, loved and feared in equal measure. One article = one worldview.
"The sea is hard, but fair. If you work, you eat."

**San Pedrito (June 29).** Mass, then the saint goes TO SEA on the patacho
(a ~12m ceremonial reed raft built for the day), escorted by caballitos,
blessing the waters. Depends materially on totora: festival beat with a
resource dependency built in.

**Superstition, handled right.** "Women aboard = bad luck" is real folklore;
let a woman character puncture it ("Dicen que traemos mala suerte. Pero sin
nosotras no hay pesca."), never the game endorse it.

**Texture.** DESERT coast: no palms, no turquoise; dunes and bare hills to a
cold rich sea. Green = totora ponds + valley fields only. Pelicans at gutting
tables, gallinazos on the market roof. Quincha construction (cane lattice +
mud plaster; Quechua word) + adobe; flat roofs; rebar sprouting; paint fading
in salt. Pier from the old sugar trade; handline fishers on it. Sounds:
surf undertone, outboard putter at 4am, wing-slaps. Smells: fishscale, lime
and cilantro at noon, wrong-wind fishmeal reek from the industrial port.

**Sierra-coast (the arrival).** 3,000 years of vertical trade: dried fish up,
chuño/charqui/wool down; trueque survives in pockets. Then 20th-c mass
migration downhill; "serrano" as everyday slur (choleo) AND deep kinship:
same yapa word, same choclo. One minor character makes the lazy serrano joke;
a migrant's daughter shuts it down; an elder remembers trading dried fish for
chuño. The player literally arrives carrying highland credibility.

**Tensions.** Industrial boats scooping inside the (weakly enforced) 5-mile
artisanal zone; El Niño as scrambler not just disaster (anchoveta flees, but
perico/langostinos/conchas arrive: change the FISH LIST in-game); maretazos
smashing boats; 2022 oil spill (women filleters "didn't exist" for
compensation); the last-generation question: Pablo Díaz, 50 years a
fisherman, now ALSO takes tourists on the caballito. Decline or survival?
Let villagers disagree.

## Traps
Tropical-beach imagery; Mexican bleed (mariachi/tacos/"amigo"); ceviche at
night or "cooked" or hours-marinated; the lazy-fisherman trope (waiting IS
work); coast-as-monoculture; caballitos as quaint props (they are working
gear replaced monthly); erasing the women who run the chain; sanitizing race;
generic "the sea"; getting garúa wrong (winter fog, not storms; rain only in
El Niño years, when it is catastrophe).

## Flagged soft claims
Boat lifespan: write "a few weeks." Heritage year: 2003. Criollo humor
register / sudado-as-cure / chongo nuance: broadly right, run past a Peruvian
reader with all coastal dialogue.
