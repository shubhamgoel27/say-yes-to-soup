# Design patterns from beloved RPGs (research pass, 2026-07-21)

Condensed working notes from a sourced research run. Full principles worth
holding the game to:

## The laws we now build by

1. **No generic nothing-text, ever.** Every examinable answers with one
   bespoke line (Mother/Undertale rule). Looking is never wasted.
2. **References must be earned**: internal to the world, situational, optional,
   never name-drops, never aimed at the player (Earthbound/Undertale synthesis).
3. **Repeatable interactions escalate.** 5+ response lines for anything the
   player can keep doing (Lesser Dog's neck).
4. **Barriers have personality** (Snorlax rule): never invisible walls; the
   excuse implies life beyond. Paca is ours.
5. **Real secrets fuel folklore**: keep at least one deep true secret so NPC
   rumors (80% true) have oxygen (Mew-under-the-truck).
6. **Puzzles come from people, not systems** (Layton): a villager's invitation,
   costless retries, friction in the thinking not the handling.
7. **Knowledge is the best key** (Outer Wilds): things open because you
   learned a fact, not because you hold an item.
8. **The world runs on its own clock / edges are doors** (Stardew's broken
   bus): every map edge should look like it goes somewhere, because it does.
9. **Juice budget**: tween everything, per-surface footsteps, emote bubbles,
   idle animations; save shake/flash for the 3-4 biggest beats.
10. **Warm fourth wall**: ask an innocent question early, pay it off at the
    end (Earthbound's prayer). Candidate: the photographer's album over the
    Chapter credits.
