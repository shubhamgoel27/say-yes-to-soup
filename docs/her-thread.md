# Her: the Nani thread

## The problem this fixes

The player walks Nani's exact 1974 route and almost nobody on it remembers
her. Counted across NPC dialogue: home mentions her 13 times and Oaxaca 26,
because those are the chapters the story already needed her in. Everywhere
between: La Caleta 0, Busan 0, Zanzibar 0, the ship 1, Kerala 1, Sicily 1.

Her journal entries taper by design (18, 13, 6, 6, 5, 6, 5, 6, 6, then
silence from mid-Sicily), and that silence is the mystery whose answer waits
in Oaxaca. But between the first page and the answer, the player learns
almost nothing new about *who she was*. She is a voice in the margins with
opinions about food, not a person being slowly reconstructed.

The goal: the further you walk, the more of her you are holding.

## The shape

**One memory per chapter, from someone who was there in 1974 or 1975.**
Not a lore dump: a person, telling you a thing they remember, in the middle
of doing something else. Each is earned, not automatic (see Gating).

They accumulate in a new journal section, **Her**, which is nearly empty when
you start and full by the time you come home. That surface is the point: the
player should be able to open the journal and *see* how much of her they now
have.

## The rule that makes it feel like a person

**Each memory should complicate the last one, not confirm it.** People are
remembered differently by different people, and a portrait assembled from
strangers should have contradictions in it. If every village says "she was
kind and curious", we have written a brochure.

The arc across the eleven beats, in play order:

| # | Chapter | What they remember | What it does to the portrait |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ch'aska Pampa | She left in a hurry and did not say goodbye properly | Sets the ache: she was loved and she still went |
| 2 | La Caleta | She paid a fisherman for a passage and then did not take it | First contradiction: she hesitated |
| 3 | The ship | She worked the galley and sang badly and would not stop | Warmth: she was fun to be near |
| 4 | Shionoura | She stayed three weeks in a town she meant to pass through | She was capable of stopping |
| 5 | Busan | She sent money home every month, and never said so in a letter | She was carrying something she did not write down |
| 6 | Kerala | She was ill here and hid it, and someone knew | She kept things from the journal |
| 7 | Delhi | She was terrible at kites and delighted about it | Relief: the portrait is allowed to be light |
| 8 | Zanzibar | She said out loud that she might not go home | The first real crack in the myth |
| 9 | Sicily | Someone saw her writing less, and asked, and she deflected | The silence gets a witness |
| 10 | Oaxaca | The telegram, the week of shelter, the debt in the ledger | The answer, already canon |
| 11 | The return | What the village decided about her leaving, said out loud at last | The player gets to answer it |

Beats 1 to 9 are new. Beat 10 exists; it is the anchor everything else must
be consistent with. Beat 11 exists in part.

## The player's own thread, braided in

The player also left. The game never says why, and it should not say it
outright, but the same surface should slowly admit it. Three or four **you**
entries, unlocked at the same beats, in the player's own hand, that are
about the player rather than about her. Not backstory: recognition.

Something like: an early one that is defensive about why unpaid leave was
reasonable; a middle one that stops defending it; a late one that admits the
journal was an excuse to have somewhere to be. The player is finishing her
book partly to avoid finishing something of their own.

Keep this light. Four entries at most. It is a chord under the melody, not
a second melody.

## Gating: earned, never automatic

A memory unlocks when the player has done the thing that makes a stranger
willing to tell them something true. In practice: after the chapter's
cooking scene, or its first warm correction, or the errand that makes you a
regular rather than a tourist. Use flags the chapter already sets.

Never gate on completing the chapter. A player who is halfway through Busan
should be carrying Busan's memory of her; the reward for engaging is the
memory, and it should arrive while there is still time to think about it.

## Voice rules

- The person telling it is doing something else while they talk. Nobody
  stops their day to deliver exposition.
- Nobody says "your grandmother was a remarkable woman". Ever.
- They should not know they are giving you something. The weight is yours.
- They remember her by a detail, not a summary: what she ate, what she got
  wrong, what she would not stop doing.
- **Her name is Zoila.** She is never called Nani by anyone outside the
  family, because Nani is what one specific child called her. Strangers used
  her name, and each village keeps it the way its own mouth does: Bi Zoila on
  the Swahili coast, Zoila-san in Shionoura, Zoila madam in Delhi, la Zoila
  in Sicily. Chapter one establishes the name; every other chapter assumes
  the player already has it.
- The moment the player first hears a stranger call her Zoila should land.
  For their whole life she has been a role. Now she is a person other people
  had opinions about.
- Lines under 150 characters, two sentences at most, no em dashes.

## Consistency: the facts these must not contradict

- She left Ch'aska Pampa westward in 1974 with a new journal.
- Her route is the player's route, in the same order.
- Her hand thins in Sicily; her last entry stops mid-sentence.
- In 1975 she spent a week in San Isidro Guelazana during Día de los Muertos
  preparations, a telegram came saying her mother was dying, she left
  overnight for Peru, and she never came back. Doña Refugio's guelaguetza
  ledger still carries the unpaid line.
- She never travelled again after that.
- She wrote postcards to the player for years, then stopped, then died.

Nothing added by this thread may contradict the above, and nothing may
*answer* the mystery before Oaxaca. Beats 2 to 9 may make the player
suspect; only Oaxaca may confirm.
